So after a long time, I'm back to gaming — but not the way I expected.

Quick context: I used to play a lot. Like genuinely a lot. Then work came in the middle and everything else piled up, and I just stopped. It went the other way completely. Back then it was mostly two games on my gaming laptop — Valorant, which is a crazy game, and Rust, which is a *super* crazy game. That was it. Those two.

And even with just those two, I used to get lag and dropped frames constantly. Like, all the time. I still played through it and it was fine back then — you kind of just accept it. But it was never smooth.

## The PC I almost bought

When I decided to get back into it, my first instinct was to build a proper rig. 32GB, something that could handle gaming *and* my AI experiments on the side. Felt like the obvious move.

Then I looked at the prices. And yeah — that plan died pretty quickly.

So I went a different route and signed up for NVIDIA's cloud gaming instead. I'd been hearing good things, people saying it's genuinely solid. Instead of dropping a crazy amount on hardware, I figured let's just try renting the hardware.

## Picking a plan

There are three tiers — Free, Performance, and Ultimate.

I actually wanted Performance. That was all I needed, honestly. But I waited one or two months and still couldn't get it, so eventually I thought: fine, let's just go extreme. Ultimate it is. High-end GPUs, the whole thing.

One real downside before you jump in — the game library is limited. You can only play supported titles, so check the list first. That's genuinely the first thing you should do, not an afterthought. My main use case was Rust, and it was supported, so I went ahead.

I'm running all this on a MacBook Air M4, by the way. More on the Mac side later.

## Actually playing

Downloaded the app, hit play, and... it just worked.

The game was running around 60 FPS for me. Which, considering what I was coming from — my old laptop three years ago was giving me 40 FPS or less and lagging like crazy — this is buttery smooth. Genuinely smooth.

And the thing that surprised me most: latency. You're basically streaming the output of a high-end PC sitting somewhere else. I fully expected input lag to ruin it. That was my main worry going in. But nope — it's quick. Quick enough that I stopped thinking about it, which is kind of the highest compliment you can give.

## The part you actually need to get right

I almost forgot the main thing: **your internet has to be good.** Not okay — good. And stable.

Ethernet is better than WiFi here, no contest. This is the one thing that will make or break the whole experience, and it has nothing to do with the service itself. If your connection is unstable, none of the rest matters.

I'm also running HDMI out to my monitor, which goes up to 200Hz, so that side is sorted too.

## The limits

You get around 100 hours per month. I mostly play on weekends, so I figured this would be fine for me.

Then I actually checked.

Nearly 70% gone, and there's still a week and a half to go before it resets. So much for "I only play weekends."

This is the part I'd genuinely underestimated. The hours don't disappear in some marathon session you'd notice — they go in two-hour evenings you forget about. Rust especially: you log in to do one thing, and then you're repairing walls for ninety minutes.

So if you play regularly — like daily — I don't think it's worth it. 100 hours works out to roughly three hours a day, and once you burn through it you're buying extra time in 15-hour blocks on top of what you already pay. That math stops making sense pretty fast. Be honest with yourself about how much you actually play before signing up — and know that you're probably going to be wrong about it, like I was.

There's also a session limit each time you launch a game. On Ultimate it's 8 hours, which is more than enough — I've never come close to hitting it. Performance is 6, and the free tier cuts you off at 1.

## About Mac gaming

Since I'm on a MacBook Air, I did try running Rust natively on macOS first. I'd seen some threads about Mac gaming getting better and wanted to see for myself.

Man. That was not good.

I'll leave it there.

## So, worth it?

Depends entirely on who you are.

If you have low specs but want to actually experience good gaming — yes, highly recommend. That's exactly my situation and it solved it. I'm getting better performance than I ever got on hardware I owned, without spending anything close to what a rig would have cost.

If you're a daily player, the hour limits will probably frustrate you more than the hardware savings will please you.

For me though, coming back after three years away and getting 60 FPS on a MacBook Air — that's a pretty good place to restart from.
